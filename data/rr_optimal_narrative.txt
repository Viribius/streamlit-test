
The Bushfire Management Strategy used long-term optimisation modelling to identify the range of annual fuel treatment required to maintain bushfire risk outcomes over time. This modelling considered factors such as fuel accumulation rates, treatment objectives, delivery capacity, and the availability of suitable burning conditions. The resulting strategic treatment ranges represent the estimated level of activity required to fully implement the strategy across all priority landscapes and treatment zones.

Since the strategy was adopted, neither Murray Goldfields nor Mallee District has consistently delivered the volume of treatment originally anticipated by the optimisation modelling. Operational constraints including limited burn windows, competing priorities, resourcing pressures, environmental considerations and seasonal conditions have reduced the ability of districts to fully achieve the strategic treatment range in many years.

Despite this, residual risk outcomes in both districts continue to track in line with, or outperform, the projected outcomes generated by the original optimisation modelling. This suggests that fuel management activities are being targeted effectively, with available resources directed towards the treatments that provide the greatest contribution to community protection and risk reduction. In practice, districts appear to be achieving greater risk reduction per hectare treated than was assumed within the statistical optimisation model.

While this demonstrates the effectiveness of risk-based planning and local decision-making, it should not be interpreted as evidence that the broader strategic treatment program is no longer required. The Bushfire Management Strategy was designed to deliver a range of outcomes beyond residual risk reduction alone, including maintaining fuel-managed landscapes, supporting suppression opportunities, and ensuring treatment is distributed across priority Bushfire Moderation Zones and Asset Protection Zones over time.

Continued organisational support, community acceptance and sustained delivery capacity will remain important to ensure the full intent of the strategy can be achieved. Encouragingly, current performance indicates that districts are consistently prioritising the highest-value treatments and achieving strong risk reduction outcomes, despite operating within a constrained delivery environment.
