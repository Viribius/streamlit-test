The Murray Goldfields district currently maintains residual risk below the district target of 75%; however, at 73%, the district is now operating with very little tolerance for poor fire seasons, delayed treatment delivery or further fuel accumulation. Residual risk has been increasing steadily as fuels recover across previously treated landscapes and, without sustained treatment, the district is likely to exceed its target within the next 12 months.

Unlike many other areas of the region, fuel management on public land has a substantial influence on bushfire risk within the Murray Goldfields. Large areas of contiguous forest occur near numerous townships and communities, meaning strategically placed planned burning can materially reduce the consequences of major bushfires. The same relationship also means that risk can return rapidly when treatment programs fail to keep pace with fuel accumulation.

Modelling indicates that, if the Joint Fuel Management Program is not delivered, district residual risk could approach 85% within three years. At that level, risk would be significantly above the district target and would represent a treatment deficit that could not be recovered through a single year of burning. Once risk reaches this point, multiple years of sustained treatment are required simply to return the district to an acceptable level of protection.

The most significant risk reduction opportunities within the current program occur across the Castlemaine and Bendigo landscapes, including strategic treatments such as Stephenson Track, Arthurs Track, Tarilta Gorge, Sugarbag, and a number of high-value burns around western Bendigo and Inglewood. These treatments collectively provide a disproportionate share of the risk reduction available to the district and are critical to maintaining residual risk below target.

The proposed program remains strongly aligned with the Loddon Mallee Bushfire Management Strategy and its implementation plan. Asset Protection Zone and Bushfire Moderation Zone burns continue to provide the greatest risk reduction benefit, while the nominated Landscape Management Zone treatments are overwhelmingly consistent with those identified through strategic planning as important risk-reduction opportunities. All treatments have been proposed in accordance with Fuel Management Zone intent, fuel hazard accumulation, ecological considerations and the applicable fire management requirements.

Delivery of the highest-priority treatments in each year of the program is fundamental to achieving the district residual risk target. Residual risk remains the department's primary public-facing measure of bushfire consequence reduction and is a key indicator of program effectiveness. Failure to deliver the highest-value risk reduction burns will significantly increase the likelihood of exceeding the district target and reduce the program's ability to demonstrate measurable reductions in bushfire consequence.

Overall, the Murray Goldfields remains below target, but only by a narrow margin. The district is entering a period where maintaining community protection outcomes will require consistent delivery of strategically important fuel management activities. The current Joint Fuel Management Program provides the best available pathway to stabilise and reduce risk over the next three years while maintaining alignment with broader landscape, ecological and fire management objectives.
