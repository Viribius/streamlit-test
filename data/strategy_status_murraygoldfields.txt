The Loddon Mallee Bushfire Management Strategy (2020) defines successful implementation of the Murray Goldfields Fuel Management Strategy as maintaining at least 50% of Bushfire Moderation Zone (BMZ) areas below their fuel hazard trigger and 100% of Asset Protection Zone (APZ) areas below their fuel hazard trigger at all times.

Unlike many districts, where fuel management performance is measured primarily through return intervals and annual treatment area targets, the Murray Goldfields strategy recognises that fuel accumulation rates vary considerably across forest types, bioregions and landscapes. This approach enables greater treatment effort to be directed towards areas of higher bushfire risk while reducing treatment frequency in lower-risk environments. In practical terms, it allows longer treatment intervals in open box-ironbark forests and open grassy woodlands, while prioritising treatment in high-risk Herb-rich Foothill Forests and areas with extreme fuel accumulation potential, such as Macedon and the Pyrenees.

The strategy is closely linked to operational burn objectives and places significant importance on field-based fuel hazard assessments. While modelling provides an important indication of fuel conditions, observed fuel hazard should always take precedence. For example, where modelling indicates fuel hazard is Extreme, but on-ground assessment identifies conditions consistent with Moderate hazard, the burn should be removed from the treatment program and prioritisation adjusted accordingly.

By accounting for the differing fuel accumulation rates across vegetation types and landscapes, the strategy estimates that an indicative annual treatment program of between 3,500 and 5,500 hectares is required, although seasonal conditions, particularly rainfall, can significantly influence fuel accumulation rates and treatment demand.

Implementation of the strategy varies across work centres. Rushworth and Maryborough have achieved particularly strong outcomes, with nearly 90% of strategic areas currently assessed as Moderate fuel hazard or below. In contrast, other areas, including Castlemaine, continue to exhibit a substantial implementation deficit, with a much lower proportion of strategic areas meeting the desired fuel hazard thresholds.
