Residual risk in the Mallee is strongly influenced by factors that cannot be materially changed through planned burning on public land. Across thousands of simulated fires, the model estimates a total impact of only 809 house losses. Most of this risk is associated with fast-moving grass and crop fires across private land, beyond the practical influence of public-land fuel management. Even if all treatable public land in the district were burnt every year, residual risk would remain at approximately 66%.

For this reason, the Mallee residual risk target is 90%, compared with the lower target used in more forested landscapes. Achieving the target requires public-land fuel management to reduce modelled impacts by approximately 10%, equivalent to around 81 of the 809 modelled house losses. Although this may appear modest, the district’s dispersed and low-density settlement pattern means that a small number of townships near large, contiguous areas of public land hold a disproportionate share of the risk that practitioners can influence.

Residual risk results in the Mallee should therefore be interpreted with care. With relatively few modelled asset losses, a single simulated fire, wind change or difference in mapped fuel can materially alter the district-wide result. This can produce apparently inconsistent year-to-year movements, including circumstances where projected risk temporarily increases with planned burning before falling again in a subsequent year. These movements may reflect modelling noise as much as an underlying change in strategic effectiveness.

In 2026, Mallee residual risk is below the district target. This reflects both the cumulative benefit of maintaining edge treatments within Priority Fuel Management Areas and the effects of recent bushfire. The Boinka–Morrison Road bushfire burnt approximately 10% of Big Desert–Wyperfeld and reduced bushfire risk in the north-east of the district, including protection outcomes for Walpeup and Patchewollock. Modelling also indicates that approximately half of the risk reduction available through public-land burning can be achieved by protecting Rainbow, Yaapeet, Brim and Beulah through treatment in the south-eastern corner of Big Desert–Wyperfeld. 

At the end of 2027, risk is modelled to rise substantially as a result of the modelling inconsistenices highlighted above. This increase is reflected across risk 1, risk 2, and local modelling systems, but is removed from the system by the end of 2028. If we disgreagard the noise, we see the Mallee will remain below its risk target over the next 3 years even if we fail to deliver our program. 

The Mallee fuel management strategy is not based on residual risk alone. It combines treatments intended to reduce fire size, protect communities and other assets, maintain habitat values, limit fires escaping from large Mallee parks, and improve opportunities for aerial and ground-based suppression. Asset Protection Zones and Bushfire Moderation Zones each perform important and complementary strategic functions. The JFMP has been aligned as closely as practicable with this broader strategic intent.

A significant program inclusion is the Year 3 Kumbrunin Boundary burn. It is ranked 21st for risk reduction across the three-year regional program, representing the first Mallee burn to rank this highly since the introduction of the risk-based fuel management approach. Modelling indicates that this treatment alone could reduce Mallee residual risk into the 70–79% range, even if no other burns in the program were delivered.

Overall, the JFMP maintains the Mallee below its 90% residual risk target while supporting a much broader set of landscape, community and suppression outcomes. The residual risk metric demonstrates the program’s contribution, but sound decisions in the Mallee require the result to be considered alongside local knowledge, operational objectives, model limitations and the wider strategy for managing fire across public and private land.
