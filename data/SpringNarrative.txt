Spring burns in the Murray Goldfields District are generally small Asset Protection Zone treatments with high coverage close to communities. Their primary purpose is to reduce fuel hazard and fuel load near homes and other assets, mitigating radiant heat exposure and reducing the likelihood of house loss. Unlike larger landscape treatments, they are not primarily intended to stop or substantially alter the spread of a landscape-scale fire.

Phoenix RapidFire modelling is an important tool for assessing and prioritising fuel management treatments. However, the Risk 1.0 framework can materially underestimate the value of small APZ burns. Its relatively coarse spatial resolution, combined with the extreme fire-weather scenarios modelled, is poorly suited to representing treatments whose principal benefit occurs over short distances at the immediate forest-residential interface.

Consequently, a low Risk 1.0 result should not be interpreted as evidence that these treatments have low value. In many cases, it reflects a mismatch between what the model measures effectively and the purpose of the treatment. Contemporary research into house loss and proximity to forested vegetation, including work by Leonard and colleagues at CSIRO, provides stronger support for managing fuels close to assets. Radiant heat modelling using HILI similarly demonstrates the potentially significant reduction in house-loss probability provided by these treatments.

The selected Spring Program remains strongly aligned with the region’s long-term fuel management strategy. It also includes two treatments with substantial benefits even under Risk 1.0: Wedderburn - Daisy Lane and Heathcote - Pannells Track. Both rank within the top 15 risk-reducing burns in the three-year JFMP and within the top six burns planned for 2026-27.

Collectively, delivery of all Spring priorities would achieve approximately 5% of the total Risk 1.0 risk reduction identified across the three-year JFMP. If the complete Spring Program were delivered, even with no subsequent Autumn delivery, residual risk would reach approximately 75.2%, close to the regional target of 75%.

Strong Spring delivery is therefore critical. It supports the annual residual-risk KPI while delivering targeted asset-protection outcomes that are not fully represented by Risk 1.0. Prioritisation decisions should consequently consider Risk 1.0 alongside Risk 2.0, radiant heat modelling, asset proximity, treatment purpose, local knowledge and operational feasibility, rather than using Risk 1.0 as a standalone measure of treatment value.
