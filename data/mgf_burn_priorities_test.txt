

Summary

This year's Joint Fuel Management Program is arguably one of the most balanced programs developed in recent years. While the ongoing absence of treatment in some high-risk catchments, particularly around Castlemaine, continues to challenge residual risk outcomes, the 2026/27 program targets risk reduction across multiple parts of the region rather than relying on a small number of locations. 

Murray Goldifelds

Combining expert-elicited nominations with outputs from the Lazarus Fuel Treatment Optimiser, the highest risk-reducing burn in the program is, somewhat surprisingly, located at Kangaroo Flat. This treatment alone is projected to reduce regional residual risk by approximately 1.8% in 2027.
 
Glenluce Wewak Track (1.7%) remains a key regional priority following delivery of Up and Down Track in 2025/26 and the addition of the new Collins Street nomination. Inglewood Potters Hill (1.5%) represents a return to risk-reduction priorities around the extreme-risk township of Inglewood, where fuel accumulation has once again reached levels requiring treatment.
 
Beyond these sites, a number of familiar high-performing treatments emerge within the Castlemaine footprint, including Stephenson's Track (1.4%), Porcupine Ridge (1.1%), and Arthur's Track (0.9%). The Cowbaw State Forest treatment at Smiths Track and the Bushfire Management Zone treatment at Heathcote Mt Ida round out the highest regional priorities.
 
Newstead Demo Track also scores surprisingly highly within the Phoenix modelling. While the operational importance of the treatment may be overstated by the model, its delivery remains important to achieving regional residual risk targets.

Mallee

In the three year JFMP the Mallee this year has four seperate burns that fall within the regions top 50 risk reducing treatments, including Kumbrunin Boundary (0.25%) which is the 21st highest risk reducing burn for the region, reducing modelled loss by 145 homes, or more than 10% of the districts risk profile.
